# WRITING: Develop a Question into a Testable Physical Answer

Use this branch for manuscript prose, introductions, conclusions, and references. [PLANNING](branch-planning.md) coordinates the target, outline, title/abstract, and layout; [FIGURES](branch-figures.md) handles graphical evidence. Follow the user's requested language. The English examples below are original illustrations of expression.

## Continue from the background, target, and argument

For a new manuscript, [PLANNING](branch-planning.md) first examines the background and closest literature, clarifies the physical question, and selects the target journal/article type. Then develop the argument and outline described below, propose a working title, plan the figures, and continue drafting. If the background, target, and outline already exist, resume from the current step. Revise the title and abstract after the complete draft. Do not add a separate Related Work section merely to fit a generic template, or impose IMRaD or one file per section.

## Establish the argument first

Extract from the actual materials: the central question; why existing explanations or methods are insufficient; the core observation or theorem; the physical claim the evidence allows; and the controls that distinguish alternative explanations. Use one internal working sentence to connect question and answer, for example: “In the specified dissipative model, which coupling channel determines the slow mode, and which spectral and dynamical controls identify it?” This does not require reducing the manuscript to a one-line conclusion and does not authorize inventing a mechanism.

Experiments, theorems, methods, and observations whose mechanisms remain unresolved require their respective kinds of evidence. Read [physics narrative](physics-narrative.md) for inference between equations or paragraphs; use the [precision audit](precision-audit.md) only for scientific contradictions.

Before outlining, use [conceptual unity](conceptual-unity.md) to select and prioritize results. State each major result's assumptions, conclusion, and role in the central question. Distinguish genuine technical dependencies, independent complementary evidence, and a merely shared topic. Deletion and splitting tests help establish priorities; do not assemble results by inventing a connecting theorem. Check mathematical novelty, scientific importance, and correctness separately. Compare the closest literature at the level of questions, assumptions, and conclusions; crowded keywords do not establish a lack of novelty.

## Abstract: develop the physical answer into a continuous argument

Require the core contribution, core method, and core reasoning to remain visible and logically connected in the final paragraph. Narrative fluency cannot compensate for a missing central method. Compress routine steps, not the decisive scientific idea or the evidence and reasoning connecting it to the result. Check both scientific rigor and natural information order.

For abstract drafting or revision, read [Abstract narrative](abstract-narrative.md): route by journal, article type, and contribution; connect the supported physical answer to its evidence and meaning; check both sentence continuity and the whole argument. Read this reference before treating the abstract as a list of required ingredients. A local edit need not rewrite valid surrounding prose.

## Introduction: develop the problem fully, rather than expanding the abstract

The introduction performs connected tasks. Let the content determine paragraph count and length; combine or expand as needed instead of imposing three paragraphs or a fixed word count.

1. **The physical problem and why it matters:** Identify the actual system, observable, or theoretical relationship, and explain how the problem affects understanding, capabilities, or existing conclusions. Broad statements such as “quantum technologies are important” cannot replace a specific difficulty. Give readers in adjacent fields the concepts needed for subsequent inferences.
2. **Existing approaches and mechanisms:** Organize the most relevant literature by physical mechanism, theoretical assumption, experimental capability, or methodological route. Explain what these approaches have already explained or achieved and where their applicability ends. Avoid a string of author names and phrases such as “extensively studied.”
3. **The precise unresolved issue:** Express the gap as a verifiable distinction between existing results and the present question, such as two unresolved mechanisms, a missing observable, a restricted parameter range, or different applicability conditions. Compare the closest work specifically; do not claim “first” or “previously unstudied” before checking.
4. **The contribution and evidence route:** Answer the preceding gap by stating the core result actually obtained and its evidential basis. Identify discriminating calculations, controls, or experimental signals. Do not preload every step and number into the introduction or substitute “this paper is organized as follows” for the contribution.

After drafting, check whether readers understand why the problem is difficult, how far prior work reaches, the exact unresolved issue, and which part this manuscript answers. If the introduction consists only of field background followed by “Here we…”, return to the materials to develop the argument and references. If the materials are insufficient, formulate specific author questions or literature searches rather than lengthening the text with generalities.

Concision removes synonymous repetition, irrelevant history, and self-evaluation; it does not remove the literature and explanation needed to understand the science. Address PRL's short format through an evidence hierarchy and a whole-manuscript space budget, rather than sacrificing the introduction's logical chain first.

## Main text: each paragraph completes an inference

Build paragraphs around a judgment, evidence, and explanation. An equation should show which term or limit produces a phenomenon; a figure should show which trend discriminates between explanations. A parameter sweep alone is not a mechanism. Causal language requires an intervention, control, or derivation. Negative results, counterexamples, and limits of applicability can be central findings.

Remove repeated qualifications already established locally. Keep necessary approximations, finite-size conditions, and the fitted nature of results next to those results. For concrete editing actions and examples, see [direct scientific prose](prl-direct-prose.md). When prose sounds templated, awkwardly translated, inflated, or excessively compressed, consult [natural scientific prose and original prompts](natural-prose.md) as needed; preserve language that is already natural.

## Proofs and terminology: explain in the order readers can understand

First explain what is to be proved, where the difficulty lies, and how the key idea resolves it. Before introducing a lemma, make clear why it is needed. Where helpful, build intuition with a simple example or limit before presenting the general proof. Exposition need not follow the proof-dependency graph mechanically: a precisely stated, defined result may be introduced first, with an explicit forward reference and its proof later. Internally, still check every dependency and assumption for circular reasoning. Every use requires an accurate statement and a traceable proof.

Prefer standard, concrete, readily understandable terminology. When a new mathematical object is necessary, provide a clear definition, notation, and an illustrative example rather than substituting an invented abstract label for explanation. Define symbols before or at first use; keep meanings, units, indices, and quantifiers consistent throughout. Figures and standalone abstracts also need sufficient definitions. Natural, elegant, readable prose comes from clear reasoning and rhythm. Restrained wit may occasionally help understanding, but do not force jokes or sacrifice precision.

## Conclusion: central question → supported physical claim → significance

Return first to the question posed in the introduction and answer it with a substantive physical claim. The claim should identify the object, relationship or mechanism, and decisive applicability conditions. It cannot be merely “we have studied X” or “the results are promising.”

Then select the key evidence needed to explain the claim, connect the observations to the mechanism or mathematical structure, and state which existing understanding or capability changes. Listing a first, second, and third result does not yet constitute synthesis. If the evidence cannot distinguish mechanisms, synthesize the established constraints or phenomena accurately rather than inventing a unique explanation.

End with the physical meaning of the result or a direct implication. State applicability conditions beside the relevant claims. Omit unrelated unstudied extensions instead of automatically turning them into an outlook or limitations section. If several explanations remain compatible with the scientific evidence, describe the actual evidence and its discriminating power accurately. The conclusion may span several paragraphs, and its final sentence need not repeat the main claim, but the conclusion as a whole must have a clear center.

Original illustration, usable only when the materials support it: “The narrow response is governed by coherent transfer into the weakly damped mode. Its spectral evolution and the delayed population transfer identify the same slow channel, linking the frequency-domain feature to energy storage in time.” Without the controls and time-domain data, the second sentence cannot be used.

After completing and checking a full manuscript, provide a separate concise substantive assessment following [conceptual unity](conceptual-unity.md). Do not turn that assessment into authorial self-defense inside the paper. First repair structural problems that can be fixed with the available materials, then deliver the judgment.

**Alignment check:** The title's object and relationship, the abstract's main result, the introduction's unresolved question, the main text's key evidence, and the conclusion's central claim should form one argument. The conclusion must not increase causality, extrapolation range, or priority beyond the evidence.

## References: usually 30–40, adjusted to the argument

The configurable default for a complete manuscript is about 30–40 references, with natural variation between manuscripts according to content. This is a planning expectation, not a minimum quota, a uniform output count, or an APS requirement. A small local edit does not authorize rewriting the full bibliography. If a complete manuscript has conspicuously few references, first check whether necessary layers of literature are missing.

Coverage may include foundational or classic work; relevant mechanisms and theory; the closest competing or preceding work; necessary experimental or platform evidence; methods actually used; and recent advances relevant to the gap. Each reference should perform a specific task. Several may jointly support one accurate general statement. Do not impose numerical allocations by category or add unrelated reviews to reach 30.

Work in this order:
- Identify gaps and claims requiring literature support, then search for direct sources. Use reviews to find leads and trace key results to the original papers.
- Verify authors, titles, journal, year, DOI/preprint version, and the cited result and its applicability conditions. Distinguish verified sources from sources awaiting checks; the latter must not masquerade as submission-ready references.
- Compare the closest work by its scientific differences, rather than announcing novelty from title or abstract keywords. Search recent work until the principal relevant approaches are covered. Stop when the argument is adequately supported, not when a numerical target is met.
- If only a few supplied references are available and searching is impossible, deliver the sourced portion and explicit gaps. Do not present three background references as a complete 30–40-item bibliography or invent plausible-looking placeholder DOIs.
- Preserve the user's valid citations. Explain substantive reasons for deleting or replacing any. Generate numbering through BibTeX/REVTeX and deduplicate preprint and published versions of the same work.

For journal-specific emphasis, consult [PRL](prl.md), [PRA](pra.md), [PRB](prb.md), [PRX Quantum](prx-quantum.md), [Physical Review Applied](prapplied.md), [Physical Review X](prx.md), or [Physical Review Research](prresearch.md) as needed.
