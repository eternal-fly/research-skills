# APS heading microtypography: choose the role before the appearance

Scope: original-research manuscripts for PRL, PRA, PRB, PRX Quantum, Physical Review Applied, Physical Review X, and Physical Review Research. Verification date: 2026-10-09. Check other article types, historical templates, and publisher proofs separately; do not apply this table indiscriminately.

**The argument determines whether a heading appears and what it is called.** Do not force Introduction, Conclusion(s), Discussion, Summary, or Outlook headings into a paper, and do not add a heading to every paragraph. Once a heading is chosen, check its run-in/displayed placement, typeface, numbering, capitalization, and punctuation precisely. A requirement for introductory or concluding content does not require printing a heading with that name.

## 1. Routing and evidence priority

For each decision, first establish `journal + article_type + heading_role`. Then distinguish body versus appendix levels, current versus historical style, and an author submission manuscript versus a production-appearance preview.

- **Current official requirements:** use the [APS Style Guide, February 2026](https://res.cloudinary.com/apsphysics/image/upload/APS_Journals_Style_Guide_Authors022326_guzwhx.pdf) as the primary reference. Headings are covered in II.G.1, PDF pp. 13–16; Note added on p. 20; DAS on p. 24; and appendices on p. 26. [Style Basics](https://journals.aps.org/authors/style-basics) calls for sections as needed and recommends consulting recent articles in the target journal.
- **Observed published style:** use this evidence to explain recent versions of record (VoRs) and differences from templates. When an observed style differs from a general rule, state both; do not promote a sample into an unwritten official rule.
- **Author/user choices:** these determine appropriate heading names, whether headings are present, and any explicitly requested historical appearance. They do not authorize fabricated scientific results, acknowledgments, or data-sharing commitments, and a user preference must not be presented as a journal requirement.

The Regular/Research route covers Regular Articles in PRA, PRB, Applied, and Research, plus Research Articles in PRX and PRX Quantum. The non-PRL Letter route covers only Letters in PRA, PRB, Applied, and Research; do not invent a PRX or PRX Quantum Letter type.

For this skill's author-selected [prose conventions](prose-conventions.md#identify-heading-punctuation-by-its-role), retain necessary run-in heading dashes and avoid optional colon-ended secondary headings when the paragraph works without them. The tables and historical examples below document heading roles; they do not authorize colons or explanatory dashes inside narrative sentences. Do not globally modify the helpers or historical examples to implement a prose preference.

## 2. Body-heading quick reference

Every entry below concerns **a heading that has already been chosen**. Sentence case must preserve the meaning of proper names, acronyms, variables, and chemical formulas; do not force mathematical content into uppercase. A run-in heading shares a paragraph with the following text, which returns to upright type.

| Route / heading role | Numbering, typeface, placement | Ending and following text |
|---|---|---|
| Current PRL principal heading | Unnumbered; paragraph indent; nonbold italic; sentence case | `Title—`, with no period before the dash; upright body text follows directly in the same paragraph |
| PRL secondary heading | Unnumbered; paragraph indent; upright | `Title:` + an em space + body text in the same paragraph |
| PRL deeper subheading | Unnumbered; paragraph indent; nonbold italic | `Title.` + a normal interword space + body text in the same paragraph |
| Non-PRL Letter principal heading | Unnumbered; paragraph indent; nonbold italic; sentence case | `Title.` + a normal interword space + body text in the same paragraph; no em dash |
| Further sublevel in a non-PRL Letter | Unnumbered; paragraph indent; upright | `Title:` + an em space + body text in the same paragraph |
| Regular/Research body level 1 | `I.`, `II.`; bold upright; uppercase; centered within the column | Displayed on its own line; body text begins a new paragraph; no additional terminal period or dash |
| Regular/Research body level 2 | `A.`, `B.`; bold upright; sentence case; centered | Displayed on its own line; body text begins a new paragraph |
| Regular/Research body level 3 | `1.`, `2.`; **bold italic**; sentence case; centered | Displayed on its own line; body text begins a new paragraph |
| Regular/Research body level 4 | `a.`, `b.`; nonbold italic; sentence case; paragraph indent | Terminal period + a normal interword space + upright body text in the same paragraph |

Typefaces and hierarchy come from the current guide. The further Letter sublevels are principally supported by official rules; the present sample does not justify claiming comprehensive visual confirmation in every journal.

### Two PRL punctuation routes that must remain distinct

- **Current principal headings** default to `Introduction—`. Checked 2025–2026 samples agree with the current guide.
- **An explicitly requested historical appearance** may retain `Introduction.—`. This form occurs in published 2023 papers and must not be denied. Do not infer a universal changeover date from the samples.
- **Note added exception:** the current guide still specifies `Note added.—`. Do not run a manuscript-wide regex that removes every period from `.—`; identify the heading role first.

When principal and secondary headings are adjacent, put them in separate paragraphs rather than squeezing two levels into one compound heading. A long run-in heading may wrap naturally; that is different from deliberately placing the heading in a separate block.

## 3. Acknowledgments, DAS, appendices, and End Matter

| Route / role | Format and placement |
|---|---|
| Current PRL acknowledgments | When there are actual acknowledgments, the visible label is italic `Acknowledgments—`, followed by upright text in the same paragraph; retain the semantic acknowledgments environment and check the rendered output |
| PRL DAS | Italic `Data availability—`, followed by text in its own run-in paragraph; after acknowledgments and related statements, before the references |
| PRL End Matter | A separate container heading after the references; centered bold upright, without a terminal dash. Do not treat it as a body run-in label |
| PRL appendices | Within End Matter; nonbold italic run-in: `Appendix—`, `Appendix A—`, `Appendix: Derivation—`, or `Appendix A: Derivation—`, with text in the same paragraph |
| Regular/Research acknowledgments and DAS | Unnumbered, displayed, centered, bold upright, uppercase; DAS follows acknowledgments and related statements and precedes appendices/references |
| Regular/Research appendices | Before the references; displayed, centered, bold upright: `APPENDIX`, `APPENDIX A`, `APPENDIX: DERIVATION`, or `APPENDIX A: DERIVATION` |
| Non-PRL Letter acknowledgments | When a visible label is used: nonbold italic `Acknowledgments.` + a space + upright text in the same paragraph |
| Non-PRL Letter DAS / appendices | **The general official rules and observed production style differ; follow the explicit routes below** |

A single appendix may omit the letter; multiple appendices use A, B, and so on. If subtitles are chosen for multiple appendices, give every appendix a subtitle. Regular appendix sublevels are `1.` (level 2, bold upright, displayed), `a.` (level 3, bold italic, displayed), and `i.` (level 4, italic, run-in). **The character `1.` alone does not identify a bold-italic third-level body heading.**

Handle author contributions, conflicts of interest, disclaimers, and other declarations under their own rules; do not style every declaration as an acknowledgment. Do not invent acknowledgment content. Check DAS requirements and wording against current submission policy and the actual research; an older sample without a DAS is not a reason to remove one.

### Non-PRL Letters: explicit DAS / appendix routes

On PDF p. 24, the 2026 guide broadly specifies a principal heading for a DAS outside PRL; p. 26 likewise describes non-PRL appendices as principal headings. Published Letters nevertheless provide examples of italic, period-terminated, run-in DAS and appendix headings. Do not claim that these two sources of guidance are identical:

- PRA 113, L030201 (2026), [DOI 10.1103/qw5d-5ztg](https://doi.org/10.1103/qw5d-5ztg), PDF p. 3: both DAS and Appendix use period-terminated run-in headings.
- PRResearch 8, L032025 (2026), [DOI 10.1103/2pg6-vd1j](https://doi.org/10.1103/2pg6-vd1j), PDF p. 5: acknowledgments, DAS, and a subtitled appendix all use period-terminated run-in headings. In [8, L032046 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1103/g2z2-2w2h), p. 5 also confirms this style for acknowledgments/DAS.
- Applied 24, L021002 (2025), [DOI 10.1103/7ktx-4h8m](https://doi.org/10.1103/7ktx-4h8m), PDF p. 6: acknowledgments, DAS, and the appendix all use period-terminated run-in headings.
- PRResearch 7, L022007 (2025), [DOI 10.1103/PhysRevResearch.7.L022007](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.7.L022007), PDF p. 5: a long appendix heading wraps naturally, with the body text still continuing in the same paragraph.

**New submission manuscript:** first follow any explicit current journal template or editorial instruction. Without a more specific requirement, follow the guide's principal-heading route, flag this difference in style, and confirm before submission. **Reproducing a checked VoR or an explicitly requested production-appearance preview:** the period-terminated run-in route may be used where the target journal has supporting evidence. Label it “observed production style,” not an official journal-specific exception. The supplied non-PRL Letter preview uses the observed run-in route and identifies the difference in sources. PRB Letter DAS/appendices were not directly verified in this review; do not unconditionally turn observations from other journals into a PRB requirement.

## 4. REVTeX and optional examples

For author manuscripts, prioritize correct class options and the native semantics of `\section`, `\subsection`, `\subsubsection`, `\appendix`, and `acknowledgments`; see [APS source-file formatting](aps-format.md). The [REVTeX FAQ](https://journals.aps.org/revtex/revtex-faq) explains that `reprint` provides only an approximate preview. Production-specific fonts, measured type sizes, and page coordinates are not additional requirements for author manuscripts.

Optional helpers and compiled examples are in [heading-examples](../assets/heading-examples/). Choose them only after establishing the target route; do not globally redefine `\section`:

| Helper | Purpose / argument convention |
|---|---|
| `\prlrunin{Title}` | Current PRL principal heading; the macro adds the em dash; the argument has no terminal punctuation |
| `\prlrunin{Note added.}` | PRL's specific period-dash exception; retain the period in this argument |
| `\apsletterrunin{Title}` | Non-PRL Letter principal heading; the macro adds a period and a normal interword space |
| `\apssecondaryrunin{Title}` | Upright secondary run-in heading; the macro adds a colon and an em space |
| `\apsdeepprlrunin{Title}` | Deeper PRL subheading; the macro adds a period and a normal interword space |

These helpers do not create section counters, table-of-contents entries, or bookmarks. They are not semantic replacements for numbered section commands.

REVTeX 4.2f's PRL `acknowledgments` environment omits the visible heading by default; that does not reproduce the current visible `Acknowledgments—` appearance. When that preview is needed, use the PRL helper inside the environment and check for duplicate labels. For non-PRL Letters, do not print both an explicit run-in acknowledgment label and a native displayed acknowledgment heading.

The native REVTeX 4.2f Regular example checked in this review renders body level 3 in italic only, unlike the guide's bold italic. The `\EnableAPSRegularThirdLevelPreview` hook in `regular-preview-hooks.tex` is a **separately enabled, local appearance preview**. Use it only for a confirmed Regular/Research route, and check compatibility before enabling it. It neither converts a Letter to a Regular Article nor replaces a submission template or globally redefines section commands.

The End Matter entry above describes verified published appearance. The five compiled examples below do not implement that full-width container. Do not claim that the output of native `\section*{End Matter}` exactly matches the production container.

The examples calibrate formatting; they are not a menu of required sections:

- `prl-chosen-headings.tex`: chooses Introduction, content-specific headings, and Conclusions, and illustrates principal, secondary, and deeper levels plus Note added. Rename the closing heading to suit the paper.
- `prl-unheaded-opening.tex`: leaves the opening unheaded, uses content-specific middle headings, and chooses Outlook for the close.
- `prl-no-main-headings.tex`: leaves both the opening and the ending without body headings; handles any required acknowledgments and DAS according to their own roles.
- `nonprl-letter.tex`: illustrates period-terminated run-in headings for the body, acknowledgments, DAS, and an appendix; the DAS/appendix follows observed production style. A paper may also omit body headings.
- `regular-production-preview.tex`: illustrates the `I.` → `A.` → `1.` hierarchy, acknowledgments, and DAS. Principal body headings may be Model, Discussion, Outlook, or other appropriate names; do not fill out a fixed IMRaD structure. See the table above for appendix formatting; this compiled example does not claim to cover appendices.

## 5. Acceptance checklist

1. Check the heading role and level before its typeface and punctuation. Do not infer formatting from the title string or numbering characters alone.
2. Do not insert a blank paragraph, `\\`, `\newline`, explicit `\par`, or a separate centered block between a run-in heading and its text. An ordinary single source-line break is not a forced layout break.
3. Allow natural wrapping. Do not lock the entire heading and first sentence into an unbreakable box or use negative spacing to force them onto one line. Do not build brittle lint rules around small differences in whether a period belongs to an italic or upright PDF span.
4. Do not turn measurements from a published sample into mandatory type sizes, proprietary font choices, paragraph spacing, or page geometry; let the template manage these. Preserve the semantics of mathematical symbols and proper names.
5. After compilation, inspect the actual pages containing the opening, each chosen heading level, the close, acknowledgments, DAS, and appendices/End Matter. The presence of a helper name in the source does not establish correct appearance.
6. Distinguish “following current official requirements,” “following checked published appearance,” “user-requested historical appearance,” and “source-only changes / compiled inspection.” Never describe an uncompiled manuscript as verified.
