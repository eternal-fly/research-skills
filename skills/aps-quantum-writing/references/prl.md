# PRL: bring the central result into view for a broad physics readership

## Abstract focus

For abstract drafting or revision, read [Abstract narrative](abstract-narrative.md): route by journal, article type, and contribution; connect the supported physical answer to its evidence and meaning; check both sentence continuity and the whole argument. Follow the matching profile there; the present file retains its other manuscript guidance.

Once section headings are chosen, consult [Heading microtypography](microtypography.md) using `journal=PRL + article_type=Letter + heading_role`. The current principal body heading is italic and run-in, `Heading—`, without a period before the dash; handle `Note added.—` as a separate exception. Headings may be renamed or omitted to suit the argument. Handle acknowledgments, DAS, and End Matter/appendices separately, without globally rewriting section commands.

For a complete introduction or conclusion, first use the [WRITING branch](branch-writing.md) to develop the problem, closest literature, and central physical proposition. [PLANNING](branch-planning.md) coordinates the title/abstract and layout.

Provide enough background to make the problem intelligible, and bring the central finding into view early. Organize the core space around the most explanatory evidence. Mathematically necessary definitions may precede the research gap, and a figure or table may carry a key comparison.

## Introduction and conclusion

When the advance is a physical relation, the close of the introduction can state it before the model. Keep a defining distinction, such as directional versus total emission, beside the relation it qualifies. For a theorem, the definition of the event being bounded can earn that space first.

In the closing passage, a law that the reader can now interpret can lead directly. When control of an experimental system makes the phenomenon visible, briefly identify what was varied and give the observed change next. Both orders work in continuous text. [PRL passages](../evidence/paragraph-order-review.md#physical-review-letters)

The reviewed examples include short abstracts, different main-text sequences, and theoretical papers with no main figures. Choose the form that serves the argument rather than prescribing sentence or figure counts. See [APS formatting](aps-format.md) for specific requirements.
