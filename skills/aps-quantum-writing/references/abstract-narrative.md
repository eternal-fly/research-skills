# Abstracts: make the physical answer intelligible

Integrated in APS Quantum Writing 1.1.0 from the user-supplied revision-3 supplement dated 2026-10-21. This reference belongs to WRITING and is called from PLANNING; it is not a fourth top-level branch. Use it for drafting, diagnosing, or revising an abstract. Load the selected journal profile below, not all seven profiles into every manuscript workflow.

## Purpose and precedence

An abstract is a short, self-contained scientific argument, not a miniature table of contents or a list of completed tasks. Its reader should understand what question is answered, what is established, and why that answer changes physical understanding or a well-defined scientific capability. Connect these elements through the actual reasoning in the paper.

Scientific accuracy comes first; current journal/article-type requirements come next. The narrative choices here are editorial recommendations informed by identified examples, not universal properties of APS articles or predictors of acceptance. Article type and contribution type matter as much as the journal. A direct result-first opening, a necessary enumeration, or an ending on a measurement can be excellent. A rigid background–gap–method–result–impact template cannot accommodate every paper.

“Physical significance” does not require a new microscopic mechanism. It can be an explanation, a new phenomenon, a distinction between physical regimes, a conceptual relation, a measurement or control capability, an impossibility result, or a resource/accuracy boundary that changes what is achievable. Translate mathematics or performance into its actual scientific consequence. Do not append an invented mechanism or a speculative application to make the result sound more physical.

## Required editorial standard: contribution, method, and reasoning together

For this skill, a substantive abstract must communicate the paper's **core contribution, core method, and core reasoning** as one coherent, rigorous narrative written for human readers. This is an explicit editorial requirement of this skill, not a claim that APS prescribes one universal abstract template.

The core contribution answers what has been established beyond the relevant prior work. The core method identifies the decisive idea, construction, reduction, measurement strategy, or analytical approach that makes the result possible. The core reasoning explains how that idea supports the conclusion. Do not present these as three disconnected inventory items, and do not omit the method to make room for a supposedly more physical story.

Distinguish the central method from routine procedures. Preserve the indispensable idea at a level a neighboring researcher can understand; omit secondary implementation details, auxiliary lemmas, and routine parameter scans when they do not carry that idea. A method name alone is insufficient when the reader cannot infer what it does. Phrases such as “a compact encoding,” “a unified framework,” or “an efficient approach” need a concrete object and function if they stand in for the actual contribution.

Explain the decisive idea by its scientific function: which obstacle it addresses, what it makes possible, and why the resulting evidence or reasoning supports the conclusion. The appropriate relationship depends on the research itself; it may be inferential, explanatory, comparative, or constructive. Do not prescribe one type of method or transfer a successful example's technical route into unrelated papers. This is a content-sensitive requirement, not a demand for extra sentences or a miniature proof.

Natural narration requires explicit scientific relationships and sensible information order, not simply more connectors. Avoid making one sentence carry a construction, a result, an interpretation, and a repeated significance claim at once. Let each sentence resolve a question raised by the preceding discussion, while retaining necessary qualifications. Do not invent a physical cause when the actual link is a mathematical implication or evidential comparison.

Use problem, key idea, supporting reasoning or evidence, result, and meaning as connected questions for the writer, not a compulsory sequence of sentences. Do not import a reference example's subject, proof structure, vocabulary, or result type into the manuscript.

Before polishing, check what is already known about the physical meaning and what this paper newly establishes. Then trace the specific contribution through the central method to the result and its interpretation. Journal-specific ordering remains applicable, including finding-before-detail guidance for PRA Letters; there is no compulsory contribution → method → reasoning sentence order.

## Inputs: extract the claim record

Read the relevant manuscript/results before drafting. Extract the following internally; do not deliver a mandatory worksheet to the reader:

- **Target:** journal, article type, readership, and whether the item is a technical Abstract, Popular Summary, or suitability statement.
- **Question:** the specific uncertainty, obstacle, conflict, or capability at stake. A field being important is not a research question.
- **Answer:** one organizing contribution or proposition, with its system/object, decisive conditions, and evidence level. A framework or platform paper may contain several dependent or complementary findings under that contribution.
- **Core method:** the indispensable idea and what it transforms, reveals, distinguishes, or makes possible. Record it separately from a list of routine tools.
- **New increment:** what previous work already established and which assumption, regime, scale, guarantee, mechanism, or capability the present contribution changes.
- **Reason:** the physical mechanism, mathematical structure, controlled comparison, or other warranted relation explaining the answer. If the mechanism is unresolved, retain an observational or constraint-based account.
- **Evidence:** the indispensable theorem, measurement, quantitative comparison, or numerical diagnostic, including the comparison baseline and demonstrated versus projected status. Distinguish what was measured from what was inferred.
- **Consequence:** what can now be understood, distinguished, measured, prepared, computed, controlled, or ruled out under those conditions.

Trace each substantive claim to an actual result, equation, figure, or documented observation. If an input is missing, use the supported narrower claim or ask a specific question. Do not fill a missing scientific link with smooth prose. A proof, simulation, hardware measurement, extrapolation, and proposal are different evidential objects.

## Compose a story, not labeled slots

1. State the question and answer in plain working language. Identify the result a reader should retain. Prioritize supporting results by their role in that answer, not by their position in the manuscript.
2. Choose the appropriate reasoning spine. Examples include obstacle → enabling structure → result → capability; competing explanations → discriminating evidence → interpretation; conjectured necessity → counterexample → revised boundary; phenomenon → controlling parameter → regimes → observable consequence. These are alternatives, not mandatory sentence sequences.
3. Choose an opening that makes the advance intelligible. A concrete physical tension often works; a result-first opening is preferable when extra background only delays an already understandable question. Do not manufacture a literature gap or assert priority without checking.
4. Present the main finding early enough that technical details have a purpose. State the core method and the reasoning that connects it to the finding. Preserve an essential method name when informative, but explain its role rather than treating the name as an explanation. Replace routine procedural detail with its scientific function where possible; do not replace the central method with a vague label or remove it for concision.
5. Attach quantitative results to what they establish. A lifetime needs a state/object and conditions; an advantage needs a metric and a meaningful comparator; a scaling law needs its resource and regime. Do not force a number into a conceptual paper or force all numbers out of an experimental paper.
6. Close on a consequence, synthesis, observable prediction, important boundary, or decisive result that follows from the argument. Significance may already be carried by earlier sentences. A separate promotional final sentence is optional, not a requirement.
7. Rewrite as one continuous paragraph unless current journal/article-type instructions require otherwise. Vary sentence length for comprehension, keep referents identifiable, and carry a scientific object from one sentence into the next. Avoid a parade of “we propose / we derive / we simulate / we discuss.” “We” and chronological connectors are not prohibited; unsupported sequencing is the problem.

## Test continuity at the level of inference

For each neighboring sentence pair, ask what the second sentence does: answers a question, gives a reason, supplies evidence, establishes a contrast, narrows a condition, or draws a consequence. Complementary evidence can support the same claim without either result causing the other. A story is coherent when these relations are visible, not when every pair contains “therefore.”

If two sentences have no identifiable relation, first check whether one is irrelevant; then clarify the shared question or reorder. Do not invent causation to join independent results. Conversely, if a missing bridge is a definition or physical interpretation, restore it rather than compressing the text further.

Check topic continuity: avoid silently changing from conditional fidelity to success probability, from a local correlation to a global entanglement measure, from bit-flip protection to coherent quantum storage, or from asymptotic query complexity to elapsed runtime. Such changes require a real argument, not a transition word.

## Journal and article-type profiles

The profiles select emphasis, not seven mutually exclusive writing styles. Consult [the evidence notes](abstract-evidence.md) for source versions and examples. For each target, retain the manuscript's real contribution type rather than rewriting its science to imitate the journal's branding.

### PRL: one consequential physical answer, understandable beyond the specialty

**Reader's question:** What specific piece of physics becomes clearer or possible, and why should an adjacent physics community care?

Bring one decisive conclusion into view. Background should create the actual question or tension, not provide a field survey. Preserve the central method, apparatus principle, or formal construction and explain how it makes that conclusion credible or intelligible; compress routine details instead. Additional findings should refine, explain, test, or delimit the same answer. Necessary technical assumptions remain even in a short abstract.

A useful route is physical puzzle → decisive answer → key evidence or enabling idea → revised understanding. A contribution-first route can be equally effective. A resource bound or experimental capability may be the physical advance; neither a new phase nor a microscopic mechanism is compulsory.

Prefer a concrete conceptual distinction over “novel,” “important,” or “promising.” If a numerical value matters, explain the boundary it crosses or the alternative it excludes. Do not force all motivation into the first sentence or all significance into the last. The inherited decoherence example ends with an additional non-Markovian diagnostic after the central significance has already been established.

**Failure to repair:** a compact list of theorems or apparatus achievements with no statement of what changes physically. Also avoid upgrading a model-specific observation into a universal claim merely to increase breadth.

### PRA Regular Article: precise physical interpretation in the relevant AMO/quantum setting

**Reader's question:** In this system and regime, what behavior, relation, or capability has been established, and what explains or validates it?

Retain the model and conditions that determine the answer. Link a formal result to quantities such as correlation, coherence, transport, uncertainty, thermodynamic work, or measurement accessibility when that is what the paper studies. Technical precision is useful when it carries meaning, not when it merely names tools.

A useful route is specific system/question → construction or finding → physical interpretation → consequence within the stated regime. Direct method-first writing is appropriate for a formalism whose defining principle fixes physically meaningful quantities. Do not add a generic “quantum technologies are important” sentence to an already self-contained technical opening.

For example, the inherited Kerr-dimer abstract connects its driving choice to attainable parameters, its correlation function to temporal resolvability, and compensation to fabrication tolerance. Each technical detail carries a consequence.

**Failure to repair:** equations, named methods, and parameter scans with no interpretation; or an artificially grand conclusion unrelated to the established contribution. A solid specialist result need not claim a field-wide revolution.

### PRA Letter: apply the article-specific editorial guidance

The current author page instructs PRA Letter authors to adhere to the structure in [Enhancing Physical Review A Letters](https://journals.aps.org/pra/edannounce/10.1103/PhysRevA.107.010001): problem in 1–2 sentences; main finding in 1 sentence; what was done in 2–3 sentences; broader context or outlook in 1 sentence. Treat this as journal/article-type-specific editorial instruction, not merely a pattern inferred from examples and not a universal APS rule. Recheck its status at submission.

Preserve the important ordering: readers meet the principal finding before the detailed work. Develop an actual logical relation between the parts; satisfying a sentence count alone does not create a story. Do not use historical Rapid Communications as evidence that they follow this later Letter guidance.

### PRB: connect a model or material to physical phenomena and observables

**Reader's question:** What happens in this material or model, which physical structure controls it, and how can the claim be distinguished from alternatives?

Name the system, degrees of freedom, regime, or symmetry needed to interpret the result. Organize observables around the same physical question: a phase distinction, excitation, response, correlation, dynamical regime, or constraint. A phase diagram is more informative when the text identifies what competes or changes across its boundaries; a fitted exponent is meaningful only with its evidential status.

A useful route is unresolved material/model behavior → decisive observable result → supported mechanism or organizing principle → prediction or implication. Numerical or methodological work can instead connect computational access to a physical quantity or regime. An unresolved mechanism calls for a carefully stated phenomenon and discriminating evidence, not an invented explanation.

State finite-size, temperature, approximation, or ideal-model conditions when they change the physical inference. A model calculation does not by itself establish an actual material phase, and finite-size trends do not automatically prove a thermodynamic transition.

**Failure to repair:** “we calculate energy, correlations, and spectra” without the physical judgment these calculations support. Separate measured/inferred order and established/suggested mechanisms.

### PRX Quantum: a capability, connection, or insight across quantum subfields

**Reader's question:** Which quantum-science bottleneck, resource relation, or conceptual connection changes, and what makes the advance useful or intelligible beyond the narrow implementation?

Give a shared entry point before dense subfield terminology. The central contribution may be an operational capability, a resource limit, a connection between theoretical frameworks, or a physical insight. Organize its conditions, construction, benchmarks, and implications around that single contribution. A hardware record and a theoretical bridge are both possible; do not force the former onto the latter.

A useful route is task or diagnostic problem → enabling idea → result and evidence → new capability or conceptual relation. For theory, translate the formal object into what it reveals. For devices, connect the metric to the operation it supports. Preserve preparation requirements, assumptions, and the difference between component performance and a complete quantum function.

The inherited boundary-scrambling example links higher-order correlation diagnostics, free cumulants, analytic predictions, and random-matrix behavior. Its physical significance is a theoretical connection, not a hardware speedup.

**Failure to repair:** platform-specific performance without an operational interpretation, or a list of mathematical tools without their conceptual connection. A long bit-flip lifetime is not sufficient evidence of coherent storage or fault tolerance.

Write the **Popular Summary separately**. Its accessible-language and length guidance must not be transferred to the technical abstract.

### Physical Review Applied: an application-relevant physical obstacle and a usable advance

**Reader's question:** Which physical limitation of a device, measurement, control task, or technological process is addressed, how is it addressed, and what capability results?

Prefer a connected chain: application-relevant physical obstacle → mechanism, identifiable feature, or design principle → strategy → supported performance or capability. Engineering details belong when they explain why the approach works or can be used. Quantitative performance should have the object, operating conditions, and relevant baseline needed for interpretation.

Evidence can be theoretical, numerical, or experimental; do not demand an experiment for every paper. A new characterization capability or diagnosis of a noise source can matter without a record performance number. Conversely, improved benchmark accuracy alone does not establish a physical application.

A useful ending is a demonstrated capability, design implication, or precisely conditioned route toward implementation. Distinguish a proposed interface, a simulated device, and a measured system. When compatibility with initialization, operations, or readout is central, explain it as a condition of usability rather than listing extra features.

**Failure to repair:** generic optimization language followed by unsupported claims about computing, communication, and sensing. State the actual use enabled by the evidence instead of appending a catalog of possible applications.

### PRX: a substantial central advance with connected layers of evidence

**Reader's question:** What important understanding, connection, framework, or capability emerges when these results are read together?

Give broad physics readers the entry point while retaining the conceptual or quantitative substance specialists need. Several results can belong in the abstract if they collectively resolve a single question: definitions and consequences, obstructions and constructions, mechanism and controls, or platform performance and its limiting physics. Do not force a long paper into a one-result slogan, but do not mistake length for synthesis.

A useful route is fundamental question or limitation → central framework/advance → logically related findings and evidence → changed understanding or capability. A framework is not mandatory; a decisive experiment or tool can be the central advance. Avoid making “paradigm shift” a required closing claim.

For platform papers, connect scale, stability, uniformity, and control to the underlying usable physical interactions. The inherited cavity-array example connects multiple engineering issues through one scalability question; demonstrated hundreds of modes remain distinct from a proposed route to many thousands.

**Failure to repair:** multiple separately impressive contributions connected only by “in addition,” or unsupported breadth obtained by removing assumptions. The **Popular Summary is a separate document**, not an expanded or simplified technical abstract with interchangeable rules.

### Physical Review Research: a clear substantive answer, with flexible contribution type

**Reader's question:** What physical question is resolved, constrained, or made accessible, and through what evidence or enabling method?

This scope includes fundamental and applied physics and technical/methodological advances. Do not require a new microscopic mechanism, broad-field breakthrough, or experimental demonstration. The contribution still needs a clear scientific object and consequence.

A useful route for mechanism papers is phenomenon → competing influences or control parameters → distinguishable regimes → interpretation. For methods papers, use physical task → structural reason the method works → validated capability and applicability. If the result is itself the clearest entry point, start there.

An ending on a new observable, a useful boundary, or a concrete prediction can be more informative than an applications claim. Necessary enumeration is legitimate when two parameters or two cases organize the physics. Explain their roles rather than deleting them merely to make the paragraph look less structured.

**Failure to repair:** disconnected parameter scans, an algorithm name standing in for the result, or upgrading a channel-specific method to arbitrary open-system simulation. Define what the advance enables within its actual domain.

## Evidence-sensitive language and compression

- Use “prove,” “derive,” “predict,” “observe,” “simulate,” and “propose” according to the work, not as stylistic synonyms. An abstract's word “demonstrate” alone does not identify an experiment.
- Keep truth-changing qualifiers close to claims. State the completed study's scope directly, such as "For local Markovian noise...", and omit lists of operations not performed or extensions not solved, whether related or unrelated. Preserve actual negative findings, uncertainty, and necessary model or evaluation conditions. Do not append "we do not claim", "remains unresolved", or equivalent qualifications; apply [direct scientific prose](prl-direct-prose.md).
- A causal explanation needs a derivation or discriminating evidence. Otherwise describe a correlation, consistency, constraint, or supported candidate explanation accurately.
- Replace “significant improvement” with the measured or proven improvement when known. Do not infer statistical significance from a descriptive adjective.
- Avoid unsupported “first,” “universal,” “scalable,” “robust,” or “quantum advantage.” Clarify the actual metric and model. A better approximation guarantee need not establish runtime speedup, and a faster subroutine need not establish end-to-end advantage.
- Delete repeated background and self-praise before deleting conditions, objects, or reasoning. Do not equate natural writing with an arbitrary ban on individual words.
- If an independent secondary result is essential, state its shared scientific purpose explicitly; otherwise move it to the body. Do not invent a theorem connecting independent claims.

## Final abstract review

The following are internal checks, not a mandatory published checklist:

1. Can the central question and supported answer be recovered without reading the paper?
2. Does the text identify the physical object/task and the significance of the actual result?
3. Does each sentence have a clear role in the argument, with identifiable referents and transitions? At the whole-paragraph level, does the answer actually address the opening question, and does the ending stay with the same contribution?
4. Are the core method and its role identifiable, with methodological detail and numbers selected for meaning rather than included merely because they occurred in the research workflow?
5. Are conditions, evidence levels, comparators, and quantities preserved?
6. Does the closing implication follow, without scope, causality, or priority inflation?
7. Does the text fit journal **and article type**, while keeping abstract, Popular Summary, and suitability statement distinct?
8. Do title, abstract, main results, and conclusion assert the same central claim?

9. Can the reader identify the core contribution, core method, and why that method supports the result from the paragraph itself? If the decisive scientific idea or its role has disappeared behind a generic label, revise before delivery.
10. Does the prose read as a continuous scientific explanation rather than separate contribution/method/result slots? Check overloaded sentences, ambiguous referents, abrupt topic shifts, redundant closing claims, and connectors without actual logical relationships.
11. Does the claimed novelty belong to this paper rather than to inherited physical interpretation or an established framework? If a secondary result appears only as “we also investigate/quantify,” either state its concrete contribution and relation to the center or leave it to the body.

A sentence-order shuffle that preserves meaning may indicate a list; it is only a diagnostic, not an automatic failure. Likewise, removing method names is a useful diagnostic only if the essential methodological substance and its role remain clear; never use this diagnostic to remove the core method. Repair scientific organization before polishing words.

Deliver a clean abstract plus only the explanation needed for the requested edit. If facts are insufficient, identify the specific missing input outside the manuscript. Do not append an audit report by default, invent missing results, or claim the revised prose improves acceptance probability.

## Format and source conflicts

Follow current official requirements rather than deriving limits from sample lengths. For these seven targets, the technical abstract is normally a self-contained single paragraph without displayed equations or tables; explain indispensable abbreviations. Normally avoid citations. If one is indispensable, follow current APS guidance for a self-contained citation rather than relying on a main-text reference number. Other APS journals/article types can have different conventions.

The February 2026 APS Style Guide, section E (PDF page 11), states general guidance of approximately 5% of article length and fewer than 500 words, and also states a PRL limit of 600 characters. This PRL wording is in tension with the length of actual published abstracts. Record the conflict: do not silently replace characters with words, infer a different hard limit from examples, or treat publication practice as permission to ignore stated instructions. Check the current journal page and submission system at submission; seek editorial clarification if the conflict remains. This narrative revision does not certify abstract-length compliance.
