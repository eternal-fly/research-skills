# Introduction endings and conclusion openings in seven Physical Review journals

## Scope of the review

On November 19, 2026, the closing passage of the introduction and the ending of the main text were inspected in 21 indexed papers, three per journal. The selection includes the 11 papers proposed for verification and ten additional papers chosen to vary the contribution or article structure. The 21 belong to the existing index of 700 distinct DOIs, with 100 per journal. These counts describe coverage. Selection was purposive, so the passages support editorial choices without estimating their frequency. [Original index](papers.csv)

Thirteen readings used the APS version of record. Eight used a fixed author manuscript matched to the published journal record. Those entries link the exact reading version separately. Section and paragraph locators refer to that version. Twenty papers had an identifiable conclusion, discussion, outlook, or closing synthesis. One Letter ended in Methods and is recorded as a structural example. In two cases the typeset pages also settled where an introduction or an ending actually stopped.

The sequence depends on what the reader already knows. A roadmap can follow a contribution, carry some of its evidence, or lead back to the scientific synthesis. An unanswered distinction can lead straight into the observation that makes it testable. At the other end of the paper, a familiar relation can lead, a new construction can introduce the capability it enables, and an unfamiliar performance criterion may need a paragraph of its own before a verdict is meaningful. The journal notes below give the actual passage order. [Editorial guidance](../references/branch-writing.md#opening-the-conclusion)

## Physical Review Letters

- **Optical depth and collective decay.** The last two introductory paragraphs give the law for total emission and then distinguish aperture-dependent detection, before the model begins. The closing synthesis again starts with the law across ordered and disordered ensembles and returns to the directional signal. The reporting verb in its first sentence introduces a physical relation. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1103/xvt4-1vg7) · [Author v2, opening paragraphs 3 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24680v2#p3) · [Closing paragraph 23](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24680v2#p23)
- **Reversible and irreversible decoherence.** Opening paragraph 3 announces the observed transition, identifies the engineered reservoir, and explains the role of which-path information. Closing paragraph 16 reverses the emphasis. It first locates the experiment, then reports what happens to coherence as more reservoir oscillators are coupled. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1103/t2yx-9bqh) · [Author v2, contribution](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15730v2#p3) · [Closing synthesis](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15730v2#p16)
- **Finite-system recurrence.** The Introduction, paragraphs 3–5, distinguishes simultaneous recurrence of all states, relates it to earlier definitions, and introduces the approximation argument. Discussion, paragraph 1, opens with the recurrence property under finite-dimensional unitary evolution and only then compares bounds for continuous and discrete time. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/392k-bgpt)

## Physical Review A

- **Photon correlations at a giant atom.** The penultimate paragraph of Sec. I introduces the bidirectional scattering treatment and the competition it reveals. The last paragraph locates the derivation and tests. Sec. VII opens with the scattering setup, then uses the correlation function to distinguish weak, strong, and intermediate coupling. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1103/w8hb-89vv) · [Author v1, Sec. I](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27506v1#S1.p4) · [Sec. VII](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27506v1#S7.p1)
- **Noise reduction under restricted control.** The late paragraphs of Sec. I attach results to section references. The final paragraph brings the quantifiers, denoising limits, and resource trade-off together. Sec. VIII opens with the constrained control problem, constructs the quantifiers, and develops their monotonicity and trade-off in the following paragraph. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1103/29q3-fq6z) · [Author v1, end of Sec. I](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14316v1#S1.p9) · [Sec. VIII](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14316v1#S8.p1)
- **Backward retrieval from a spin-wave memory.** Sec. I, paragraphs 2–3, identifies reabsorption and geometrical constraints, introduces Stark control, and gives the measured conditional fidelities. Sec. V opens with achieved backward retrieval at full optical depth with coherent noise suppression. It explains the phase control without repeating the fidelity number. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/j9v5-fsbc)

## Physical Review B

- **Geometric decoherence time.** Sec. I defines the loss of the entropy–negativity relation, distinguishes a later negativity peak, and develops two settings before the roadmap. Sec. V begins with the diagnostic and then identifies the weakly dissipative topological regime in which the peak can overestimate the onset. The next paragraph distinguishes a long-time result from numerical tracking during evolution. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1103/d5f3-r8d2) · [Author v2, Sec. I](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02743v2#S1.p4) · [Sec. V](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.02743v2#S5.p1)
- **Observable detection of the Mpemba effect.** The introduction moves from the cost of repeated state tomography to a condition on an observable. The run-in Summary reintroduces the detection difficulty before giving the alternative for Markovian dynamics and the local-observable examples. The opening can earn its place by making the experimental question legible. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1103/m4wy-kdc9) · [Author v1, opening](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02709v1#p3) · [Run-in Summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02709v1#p16)
- **Plasmon linewidths in a Josephson chain.** Sec. I, paragraphs 4–7, contrasts equilibrium with driven modes, predicts how scattering affects the linewidth, and then gives the section guide. Sec. V A starts from the scattering process and its linewidth signature. Its next paragraphs follow the body order because going from equilibrium to pumping changes the physical question. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/cw3m-thsw)

## Physical Review Applied

- **End-to-end quantum run time.** The end of Sec. I pairs case studies with operational comparisons and classical baselines. Sec. VI, paragraph 1, defines practical advantage through time to solution. Paragraph 2 gives the negative verdict for the demonstrations and baselines considered. The paper then explains the individual comparisons. The criterion and verdict occupy different paragraphs. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1103/gpsf-pn1x) · [Author v3, Sec. I](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06337v3#S1.p5) · [Sec. VI](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06337v3#S6.p1)
- **Shared-line qubit control.** The end of Sec. I moves from interference to pulse spacing and the calculated gate fidelity. Sec. VII, paragraph 1, recalls the single-line analysis, explains the benefit of orthogonal spacing, and reports comparable fidelity at half that spacing in the studied settings. The support is analytical and numerical. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1103/fmqf-w6ht) · [Author v2, Sec. I](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16855v2#S1.p4) · [Sec. VII](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16855v2#S7.p1)
- **Silicon-carbide magnetometry.** Sec. I, paragraphs 3–4, explains how a waveguide addresses optical collection and identifies the continuous-wave and pulsed measurements. Sec. IV opens with the sensor approach, then gives its measured sensitivity gain against confocal measurements. The numerical comparison earns its place in the conclusion. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/w49h-wfck)

## PRX Quantum

- **Finite-size quantum key rates.** Sec. I, paragraphs 4–5, presents the cones used to compute key rates, then locates the security analysis, numerical examples, and benchmarks. Sec. VII first specifies the security setting and the optimization enabled by the two cones. Protocol-specific runtimes and rates follow in paragraph 2. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/bf9s-m4jb)
- **Multimode phase transitions.** Sec. I, paragraphs 4–5, identifies the observed transition and the two ways of locating it before mapping the measurements. Sec. VI opens with the observation in a 21-resonator chain. Transmission, noise spectra, and switching times then have distinct jobs. The spectral and lifetime estimates agree on the transition line, while the mean-field comparison has a stated power range. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/rvhv-ms4t)
- **Boundary scrambling.** Sec. I, paragraphs 4–6, describes the construction and why it permits an exact calculation. Its section guide also explains why the extension to higher orders requires additional proof. Sec. V opens with the circuit and the higher-order correlations that become solvable, then connects the exact dynamics to frequency-resolved correlations. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/6n9v-b3b7)

## Physical Review X

- **Nematicity on a distorted kagome surface.** The last paragraph of Sec. I leaves the origin of nematicity open. Sec. II first identifies the exposed surface and then presents its spectroscopic signature. The full mechanism emerges later. The last paragraph of Results joins the calculated mechanism with real- and reciprocal-space observations. The introduction-to-Results transition is visible on printed page 031069-2 and the closing synthesis on pages 031069-8 and 031069-9. [APS article and typeset passages](https://journals.aps.org/prx/pdf/10.1103/2jst-psds)
- **A 600-site cavity array.** Late in Sec. I the achieved optical characteristics and projected networking performance precede a roadmap organized by field of view, loss, and degeneracy. Sec. VI, an Outlook, first returns to the achieved array performance and its measured limitations. Greater networking bandwidth with a processor is a projection. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/9cwp-ppp2)
- **Out-of-time-ordered transport.** Sec. I, paragraphs 5–6, asks which correlations conventional transport can predict, gives a qualified answer, and assigns different jobs to a scaling argument, a field theory, and numerics. Sec. VI opens with the additional transport parameters predicted by the theory and then identifies their numerical extraction in one-dimensional models. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/b5vq-s853)

## Physical Review Research

- **Spectral noise reduction in phase sensing.** Sec. I, paragraphs 2–3, asks what quantum correlations improve under matched technical noise and identifies the peak and background as distinguishable contributions. Sec. V, paragraph 1, reports a 3 dB signal-to-noise improvement under that comparison. It assigns the gain to the lower noise floor and records the unchanged peak. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/27hr-smgc)
- **Energy relaxation in fluxonium.** The last paragraph of Sec. I gives the frequency-dependent relaxation measurement, the loss model, and the effective quality factor used to compare devices. Sec. VII opens with the eight qubits and two fabrication processes, then explains the model-based conversion to that comparison quantity. The interpretation of the loss sources follows in paragraph 2. [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/3n9q-lg8m)
- **Thermal Rydberg linewidth.** The opening run-in Introduction on page L032046-1 corrects an earlier linewidth estimate, then identifies the analytic line shape and its experimental test. Methods ends the main text on page L032046-5 with the angle calibration and uncertainty convention immediately before Acknowledgments. This supplies a structural boundary case, with no conclusion opening to classify. [APS article and typeset passages](https://journals.aps.org/prresearch/pdf/10.1103/g2z2-2w2h)
